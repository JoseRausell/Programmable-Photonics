1 · Geometry and round trip

Round-trip length (a ring has Lc = 0):

L = 2πR + 2Lc

Round-trip phase, with an effective index that varies with wavelength through the group index (the reference is λref = 1550 nm):

φ(λ) = 2πλ neff(λ) L + Δφth,   neff(λ) = neff − (ng − neff)λ − λrefλref

Round-trip field transmission, with the loss α given in dB/cm:

a = 10−αL/20

2 · Add-drop transfer functions

Two lossless couplers: r1, κ1 on the input bus and r2, κ2 on the drop bus (ri² + κi² = 1). Light reaches the drop bus after half a round trip:

t = r1 − r2a e−jφ1 − r1r2a e−jφ,    d = −κ1κ2√a e−jφ/21 − r1r2a e−jφ
T = r2²a² − 2r1r2a cosφ + r1²1 − 2r1r2a cosφ + (r1r2a)²,    D = (1 − r1²)(1 − r2²) a1 − 2r1r2a cosφ + (r1r2a)²

At resonance (φ = 2πm):

Tmin = (r1 − r2a)²(1 − r1r2a)²   Dmax = (1 − r1²)(1 − r2²) a(1 − r1r2a)²
PringPin = κ1²(1 − r1r2a)²

4 · Coupling regimes

From the input bus, the drop coupler acts as an extra loss: the effective round-trip transmission is r2a.

Critically coupled (r1 = r2a): Tmin = 0. All the light either goes to the drop port or is lost in the ring.

Under-coupled (r1 > r2a) or over-coupled (r1 < r2a): the through dip doesn't reach zero.

5 · Backscattering and resonance splitting

Sidewall roughness reflects a small part of the light into the counter-propagating mode. Here it is modelled as a point reflector inside the ring, with field reflection ρ and transmission τ = √(1 − ρ²). The forward and backward modes couple, and the round trip becomes a 2×2 matrix:

ae−jφ [ τ   jρ ; jρ   τ ]  →  eigenvalues  τ ± jρ = e±j·asin ρ

The standing-wave supermodes resonate at φ = 2πm ± asin ρ, so each resonance splits:

Δλsplit = FSR · asin ρπ

The doublet is resolved when Δλsplit > FWHM, so high-Q rings show splitting first. Part of the light comes back to the input port (purple curve) and leaves through the add port of the drop bus (orange curve).

6 · Thermal tuning

A heater on the ring adds a phase proportional to the dissipated electrical power:

Δφth = π PPπ,   P = I²Rh

Every resonance red-shifts by the same fraction of the FSR:

Δλres = FSR · Δφth2π  →  dλdP = FSR2Pπ

A power of 2Pπ shifts the comb by exactly one FSR. With the laser fixed at λ₀, sweeping the current scans the resonances across the laser: that is the “single λ” mode.
