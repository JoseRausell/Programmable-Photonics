Theory

1 · Absorption in germanium

Silicon is transparent at 1.3–1.55 µm, so light is absorbed in germanium grown on top of the silicon waveguide. The light leaks evanescently into the Ge and decays exponentially along the device:

P(z) = P0 e−Γαz,    ηabs = 1 − e−ΓαL

Γ is the fraction of the mode inside the Ge and α its absorption coefficient. Ge on Si has a slight tensile strain that moves its direct band gap to ≈ 0.77 eV (≈ 1.6 µm), so α drops sharply above 1.55 µm. Heating shrinks the gap and extends the response to longer wavelengths.

2 · Responsivity and quantum efficiency

Every absorbed photon creates one electron–hole pair, collected by the electric field of the PIN junction:

R = IphP = η q λh c ≈ η λ [µm]1.24 A/W

η = ηc ηabs includes the coupling into the detector. The ideal limit at 1.55 µm is 1.25 A/W. Longer devices absorb more, but have more capacitance and dark current.

3 · Bandwidth

Two effects limit the speed: the carriers need time to cross the intrinsic region of thickness d, and the junction capacitance must be charged through the series and load resistances:

ft ≈ 0.45 vsatd,   fRC = 12π (Rs + RL) C,   1f3dB² = 1ft² + 1fRC²

with C = εA/d + Cpad . A thin intrinsic layer is fast for transit but slow for RC: there is an optimum thickness.

4 · Dark current and noise

Defects at the Ge/Si interface generate a dark current Id ∝ area that grows with reverse bias and temperature. The detected current fluctuates because of:

σ²shot = 2q(Iph + Id)B,    σ²th = 4kBT BRL,    SNR = Iph²σ²shot + σ²th

At low power the thermal noise of the load dominates (SNR ∝ P²); at high power the shot noise of the signal dominates (SNR ∝ P).
