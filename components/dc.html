Theory

1 · Supermodes and coupling coefficient

Two waveguides placed close together form two supermodes, one even and one odd, with slightly different effective indices ne and no. Light launched in one waveguide excites both, and their beating moves the power from one guide to the other:

κ = π (ne − no)λ,    Lx = π2κ = λ2 (ne − no)

The evanescent overlap decays exponentially with the gap, and the mode expands at longer wavelengths. Typical fit used here: κ = κ0 e−(g−g0)/γ (λ/1.55 µm)p

2 · Coupled-mode equations

dAdz = −jδA − jκB,    dBdz = +jδB − jκA,    δ = β1 − β22 = πΔnλ

With light in waveguide 1 only, and Ω = √(κ² + δ²):

Pcross = κ²κ² + δ² sin²[Ω (L + L0)],    Pbar = 1 − Pcross

L0 is the extra coupling in the input and output bends. If the guides are not identical (δ ≠ 0), complete transfer becomes impossible: the maximum is κ²/(κ² + δ²) .

3 · Transfer matrix and link to the MZI

For identical guides the coupler is a 2×2 unitary matrix, with θ = κ(L + L0):

MDC = cos θ−j sin θ−j sin θcos θ

A 3 dB coupler needs θ = π/4. Any deviation is exactly the imbalance α of the MZI model, M(α), with θ = π/4 − α:

Because κ depends on the gap and on λ, a directional coupler is compact but sensitive to fabrication and narrow-band, unlike an MMI.
