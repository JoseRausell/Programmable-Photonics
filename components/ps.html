Theory

1 · Thermo-optic effect

Heating changes the refractive index of silicon, dn/dT ≈ 1.86·10⁻⁴ K⁻¹. A temperature rise ΔT over a length L gives a phase shift:

Δφ = 2πλ dndT ΔT L

2 · Heat conduction in the cross-section

The heater is a line source dissipating q = P/L (W/m) on top of the oxide (k ≈ 1.4 W/m·K). The substrate acts as a heat sink (ΔT = 0) and the top surface is adiabatic. Solving ∇²T = 0 with the method of images (the infinite series has a closed form) gives:

ΔT = q2πk ln sinh²A + sin²B+sinh²A + sin²B−,   A = π(x − x0)4H,   B± = π(y ± H)4H
ΔT(x, y) = G(x, y) · P/L

H is the total oxide thickness (BOX + d) and x0 the source position. Far from the heater ΔT decays as exp(−π|x|/2H). The heater width is integrated numerically. Geometry factor at the core:

3 · Pπ does not depend on the length

Combining both equations, the length cancels:

Pπ = λ2 (dn/dT) Gcore

A longer heater reaches the same phase with a lower temperature and current density (better reliability), but needs the same power. Pπ is set by how efficiently heat reaches the core: the heater–core distance, the oxide thickness and the lateral heat spreading.

4 · Isolation trenches

Deep trenches etched next to the waveguide block lateral heat flow. Here they are modelled as adiabatic walls at x = ±a, which adds mirror images in x. Heat is forced to flow down towards the core, so Gcore grows and Pπ drops. Removing the substrate under the heater (undercut) reduces Pπ much further, but slows the response.

Ideal trenches also isolate the neighbouring waveguides completely (zero crosstalk outside the trenches). In real devices some heat still couples through the substrate.

5 · Thermal crosstalk

A neighbouring waveguide at distance s also heats up and picks up an unwanted phase:

XT = ΔφneighbourΔφown = G(s, ycore)G(0, ycore)

In dense programmable meshes this crosstalk has to be calibrated (a crosstalk matrix) or reduced with trenches and larger spacing.

6 · Time response

The slowest thermal mode of an oxide stack of thickness H (sink below, adiabatic top) gives a first-order response:

τ ≈ 4H²π² αth,   αth = kρcp,   f3dB = 12πτ

With overdrive (pre-emphasis), the heater is driven at k·P for t1 = τ ln(k/(k−1)) and then set to P, so the target phase is reached at t1 instead of after several τ.
