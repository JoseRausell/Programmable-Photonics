Theory

1 · Subtracting two photocurrents

Two photodiodes are connected in series between +V and −V. The middle node collects the difference of their currents, which goes to a transimpedance amplifier (TIA):

Iout = R1P1 − R2P2

Whatever reaches both diodes equally (the common mode) cancels; only the difference survives. A large DC photocurrent never reaches the amplifier, so it can have high gain without saturating.

2 · Interferometric detection with a local oscillator

A weak signal Es and a strong local oscillator ELO are mixed in a 3 dB coupler. The two outputs carry the interference term with opposite signs:

P1,2 = Ps + PLO2 ± √(PsPLO) cos Δφ  ⇒  Iout = 2R √(PsPLO) cos Δφ

The signal is amplified by the LO (√PLO), the phase Δφ becomes measurable, and the large DC terms cancel. Balanced detection gives twice the signal current of a single photodiode (+6 dB in electrical power).

3 · Common-mode rejection ratio

Cancellation is perfect only if both arms are identical. With a responsivity mismatch (R1 ≠ R2) or an unbalanced coupler (split 1/2 ± ε), part of the common mode leaks to the output:

CMRR = 20 log10 R1b² + R2a²|R1b² − R2a²|,   a², b² = ½ ± ε

A 1 % mismatch already limits the CMRR to ≈ 46 dB.

4 · Noise: why balanced detection wins

σ²RIN = (kcmPLO)² · RIN · B,   σ²shot = 2q(I1 + I2)B,   σ²TIA = in²B

The intensity noise of the LO (RIN) is common to both diodes and is suppressed by the CMRR (kcm = R1b² − R2a²). Shot noise is not correlated and always adds. With enough LO power a balanced receiver reaches the shot-noise limit, SNR = 2R Ps/(qB), whereas a single photodiode is eventually limited by RIN.
