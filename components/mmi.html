Theory

1 · Modes of the multimode section

The wide section of width W guides M lateral modes. With the effective width We (which includes the penetration of the field into the cladding, TE polarization):

We = W + λπ (nr² − nc²)−1/2,   ψν(x) ∝ sin(ν+1)π (x + We/2)We,   βν = √(k²nr² − ((ν+1)π/We)²)

The beat length of the two lowest modes sets every imaging distance:

Lπ = πβ0 − β1 ≈ 4 nr We²3 λ,   β0 − βν ≈ ν(ν+2)π3Lπ

2 · Self-imaging

The input field is expanded in the modes, cν = ∫ Ein ψν dx. Each mode then accumulates its own phase:

E(x, z) = Σν cν ψν(x) ej(β0 − βν)z

Since the phases go as ν(ν+2), they realign periodically and the input is reproduced as one or several images:

  • General interference (any input): N images at z = 3Lπ/N. A 2×2 coupler at 3Lπ/2.
  • Paired interference (inputs at ±We/6, modes 2, 5, 8… not excited): N images at Lπ/N. A shorter 2×2 at Lπ/2.
  • Symmetric interference (centred input, only even modes): N images at 3Lπ/(4N). A 1×2 splitter at 3Lπ/8, with outputs at ±We/4.

The exact (non-paraxial) βν used here makes the higher modes slightly mismatched, so the images are not perfect. Modes beyond cut-off are lost, which adds excess loss.

3 · Outputs, phase and link to the MZI

The output amplitudes are the overlaps of E(x, L) with the output waveguide modes. An ideal 1×2 splits 50:50 in phase. An ideal 2×2 splits 50:50 with a 90° difference:

Since Lπ ∝ We²/λ, a width error shifts the imaging point by δL/L ≈ 2δW/We. The splitting changes slowly with λ, so an MMI is more broadband and fabrication-tolerant than a directional coupler, at the cost of a larger footprint.
